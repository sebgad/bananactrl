## Mains side

The machine keeps its stock protection chain; only the brew thermostat is replaced by the SSR (the
Pt1000 sits where the brew thermostat was or next to it, see README "Software steam switch"). The brew
switch S2 has two poles (pump, valve); the hot-water switch S3 runs the pump only, and with K2 parallel to the
pump it also signals "pump on" (see open points).

| Part | Requirement |
|---|---|
| SSR | zero-crossing, DC control 3–32 V, ≥ 25 A rated for a ~4–5 A load (derating), on a heat sink: ~1.2 V drop × ~4.8 A ≈ 6 W |
| Safety thermostat | stays in series and untouched: SSRs usually fail *shorted*; then the steam thermostat limits the boiler, and the safety thermostat backs it up |
| Steam thermostat | stays in series: the hardware steam switch bypasses the SSR, the bimetal then ends heating (≈ 119 °C at the Pt1000) |
| Pump-detect relay | coil rated for 230 V AC, in parallel with the pump; contact rated for 3.3 V signal level (gold-plated or reed type: no wetting current) |
| AC/DC module | 5 V ≥ 1 A (Wi-Fi peaks ~0.5 A), reinforced insulation (e.g. HLK-PM01 class), input fuse |

Wire mains connections with heat-resistant insulation (silicone or PTFE) and keep the low-voltage board
away from the boiler.

## Low-voltage side

### Power

| Net | Source | Loads |
|---|---|---|
| 5 V (`VUSB`) | AC/DC module via terminal `PSU1` | ESP32 board (its own LDO), LED header pin 6, bridge supply (firmware assumption, see below) |
| 3.3 V | ESP32 Thing Plus LDO | ADS1115 VDD, I2C/ALERT pull-ups, bridge supply on the V1 PCB |
| GPIO 26 | driven high at boot (`kSensorSupply`) | "3.3 V source for the sensor circuit"; not connected on the V1 PCB |

A USB cable and the AC/DC module must not feed `VUSB` at the same time unless one of them goes through a
diode; unplug the module (or the cable) while flashing over USB.

### Temperature: Pt1000 bridge and ADS1115

See sheet 2. ADS1115 0x48: ADDR → GND, VDD 3.3 V with C1 100 nF; SDA → GPIO 23, SCL → GPIO 22,
ALERT/RDY → GPIO 14 (10 kΩ to 3.3 V each, plus the internal pull-up on GPIO 14).

- Firmware: differential AIN0−AIN1, PGA ±0.512 V, continuous, 8 SPS, ALERT/RDY as conversion-ready pulse
  (config `0x880C`). The bridge reads 0 V at ≈ 77 °C (Pt1000 = 1298 Ω); −0.324 V at 0 °C, +0.269 V at
  160 °C, so the ±0.512 V range fits with margin.
- Resolution at the brew temperature: ≈ 3.5 mV/K, LSB 15.6 µV → ≈ 0.005 K per count.
- Sensor current at 5 V: ≈ 1.9 mA, ≈ 4.8 mW in the Pt1000. Its self-heating is a constant offset in water,
  absorbed by the calibration, but stays about 2.3× smaller on 3.3 V.
- Supply sensitivity: the ADS1115 uses an internal reference, so the reading is *not* ratiometric. 1 % on
  Vbridge shifts the reading by ≈ 0.16 K at 93 °C (≈ 0.5 K at 20 °C); a ±5 % USB-grade supply gives up to
  ±0.8 K. This is why the 5.0 V and 5.08 V tables differ.
- Input range: ADS inputs must stay within GND − 0.3 V … VDD + 0.3 V (3.6 V). With 5 V on the bridge the
  midpoints sit at 2.2–2.8 V, which is fine, but a shorted Pt1000 puts 5 V on AIN1 through no resistance.

The resistor values on the V1 PCB are 1310 Ω, the firmware's tables use 1298 Ω (`tools/calc_wheat_stone.py`).
Use 0.1 % resistors with a low TC (≤ 25 ppm/K): a 0.1 % mismatch between R1/R2 alone is ≈ 1.3 mV ≈ 0.4 K.

Spare inputs: AIN2/AIN3 of 0x48 are on terminals `SENS3`/`SENS4`; the V1 PCB also has a second ADS1115
at 0x49 (ADDR → VDD, ALERT → GPIO 32) with a second bridge for `SENS2`. The firmware uses 0x48 only.

### Heater SSR

GPIO 21 → terminal `SSR1` (+), GND → `SSR1` (−). LEDC PWM, 15 Hz, 8 bit (`SsrFreq`, `PwmSsrResolution`).

- A zero-crossing SSR switches only whole half-waves: one 66.7 ms period holds 6.7 half-waves at 50 Hz, so a
  single period has ~7 effective steps. The boiler's thermal mass averages this; finer duty needs a lower
  PWM frequency, not more bits.
- The GPIO drives the SSR input directly (3.3 V, typically 5–12 mA). For an SSR whose input needs more than
  ~3 V or ~10 mA, add an NPN/N-MOSFET low-side driver from 5 V.
- Boot state: GPIO 21 is not a strapping pin and floats until the firmware configures it. A 10 kΩ pull-down
  on the SSR input keeps the heater off during reset and flashing.

### Pump detection

GPIO 17, internal pull-down, interrupt on both edges, debounced 200 ms in software. High = pump runs.
Wiring: relay contact between 3.3 V and GPIO 17; the relay coil in parallel with the pump (230 V AC).

The V1 netlist leaves GPIO 17 unconnected, so this input was added after the PCB. A 1 kΩ series resistor and
a 10 kΩ external pull-down make the long wire to the relay less sensitive to interference from the pump.

### Status LEDs

| Signal | GPIO | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Red | 13 | LEDC 500 Hz, 8 bit, active high (common cathode or low-side driven) |
| Green | 27 | same |
| Blue | 12 | **strapping pin (MTDI, flash voltage)**: must be low at reset, otherwise the module boots with 1.8 V flash and fails. Keep the LED/driver from pulling it high. |
| Status | 33 | green on-board LED |

On the V1 PCB these leave on the 6-pin header `J1` ("Relay_Board"): 1 GND, 2 GPIO 13, 3 GPIO 12,
4 GPIO 27, 5 GPIO 33, 6 VUSB. Series resistors sit on the LED board; size them for ≤ 10 mA per GPIO.

## Open points

1. **Bridge supply voltage.** The firmware default (`BANANA_PT1000_LOOKUP_5V0`) says the installed bridge
   runs on 5 V; the V1 netlist powers it from 3.3 V. Measure Vbridge on the machine and record it here.
2. **GPIO 26 "sensor supply".** What does it power on the installed board? 3.3 V at ≤ 12 mA from a GPIO is
   enough for the ADS1115, not for a 5 V bridge.
3. **Resistor values** 1298 Ω (firmware tables) vs. 1310 Ω (V1 PCB). A 1 % difference in all three bridge
   resistors moves the zero crossing by ≈ 3 K; either fit the table to the real values or measure them.
4. **Pump-detect wiring.** Relay type, and whether the hot-water switch also triggers it (it would start the
   brew feed-forward while drawing hot water).
5. **SSR model** and its input current at 3.3 V; heat sink; whether a pull-down is fitted.
6. **AC/DC module** model, its fusing and placement.
7. **Machine model/year** (heater power, thermostat ratings); the wiring above follows the usual Silvia
   layout in which the steam switch bypasses the brew thermostat.

## Suggested improvements (next PCB)

- Ratiometric measurement: measure Vbridge on AIN2 through a 10 kΩ/10 kΩ divider (2.5 V, single-ended,
  PGA ±4.096 V) and divide the bridge voltage by it in software, which removes the supply dependence; or
  power the bridge from a precision reference.
- 1–4.7 kΩ series resistors plus 100 nF differential / 10 nF common-mode capacitors on AIN0/AIN1: an RC
  filter against SSR and pump noise that also limits the current of a shorted sensor.
- Pull-down on the SSR input; NPN/MOSFET SSR driver from 5 V.
- Optocoupler (e.g. AC-input PC814 with a series resistor from mains) instead of the relay for pump detection,
  with a separate input for the hot-water switch.
- Move the blue LED off GPIO 12 (strapping pin), e.g. to GPIO 25.
- Schottky diode between the AC/DC module and `VUSB` so USB flashing with the machine on is safe.
- TVS / ESD protection on the sensor terminals; strain relief for the Pt1000 cable.
